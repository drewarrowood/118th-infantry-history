# Brancourt and Related Citations

## Capture of Brancourt-le-Grand (8 October 1918)

The 118th Infantry Regiment (30th Division) captured Brancourt-le-Grand on 8 October 1918 as part of the post-Hindenburg Line advances (Hundred Days Offensive / Battle of Montbrehain phase). The regiment advanced roughly 5,000 yards that day and took 766 prisoners (16 officers). Company E participated; Private Robert L. Dorton (Co. E) was cited for assisting in the capture of 53 prisoners from enemy dugouts near Brancourt.

A memorial stele in the village honors the 118th Infantry, 59th Brigade, 30th Division for liberating it on 8 October 1918 after more than four years of German occupation.

## Private Robert L. Dorton – Silver Star Citation

Private Robert L. Dorton (ASN 1310957), Company E, 118th Infantry Regiment, 30th Division.  
Near Brancourt, France, 8 October 1918.

From GHQ, A.E.F., Citation Orders No. 2 (3 June 1919):

> By direction of the President, under the provisions of the act of Congress approved July 9, 1918 (Bul. No. 43, W.D., 1918), Private Robert L. Dorton (ASN: 1310957), United States Army, is cited by the Commanding General, American Expeditionary Forces, for gallantry in action and a silver star may be placed upon the ribbon of the Victory Medals awarded him.
>
> Private Dorton distinguished himself by gallantry in action while serving with Company E, 118th Infantry Regiment, 30th Division, American Expeditionary Forces, in action near Brancourt, France, 8 October 1918, in assisting in the capture of fifty-three prisoners from enemy dugouts.

## Medal of Honor Recipients (118th Infantry, 8 October 1918)

- **1st Lt. James C. Dozier**, Company G – Near Montbrehain. Continued leading despite shoulder wound; assaulted machine-gun nest with grenades and pistol; captured prisoners from a dugout.
- **Sgt. Gary Evans Foster**, Company F – Near Montbrehain. Single-handedly attacked machine-gun nest in a sunken road; killed several and captured 18.
- **Sgt. Thomas L. Hall**, Company G (KIA) – Near Montbrehain. Overcame two machine-gun nests; advanced alone and killed five with bayonet; mortally wounded attacking another nest.

These men were in Companies F and G; Dorton was in Company E. Same regiment and battle, different companies.
